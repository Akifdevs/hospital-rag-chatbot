# Midnight Cinema: A Review of *The Last Lantern*

*The Last Lantern* is a quiet science-fiction film about a lighthouse keeper living on a moon where the sun rises only once every eighteen years. Rather than relying on giant space battles, the movie builds its story around loneliness, routine, and the strange comfort of waiting.

## What Works

The strongest part of the film is its atmosphere. Nearly every scene is wrapped in blue-gray darkness, with the lighthouse serving as the only warm source of light. The visual design makes the moon feel both beautiful and slightly unsettling.

The soundtrack is equally restrained. For long stretches, there is almost no music at all. Instead, the audience hears wind, machinery, footsteps, and distant radio signals. This makes the rare musical moments feel surprisingly emotional.

## The Story

The plot follows Mara, a technician who maintains the lighthouse after the rest of the settlement has been evacuated. One night, she receives a transmission from a ship that supposedly disappeared decades earlier.

The mystery is interesting, but the movie is less concerned with explaining every detail than with asking what Mara will do when she discovers that someone may still be listening.

## Final Thoughts

*The Last Lantern* is not a fast movie, and viewers expecting conventional science fiction may find it unusually slow. However, its patience is part of its identity.

The film ultimately feels like a meditation on communication: sometimes the most important message is not what someone says, but the fact that they answered at all.

**Rating:** 8/10
